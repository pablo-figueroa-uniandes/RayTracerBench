## Project status

The "Projective Matrix" stage's diagram was reworked after the user compared it against a classic
eye/sight-line/picture-plane perspective illustration (the kind found in perspective-drawing
textbooks — an eye, straight sight lines to an object, a picture plane the lines pass through with
the flattened result traced on it, and a ground/reference plane) and found the original version
didn't match: it drew fixed frustum edges to the *image plane's own* corners (unrelated to the
scene's actual geometry) and a dense per-vertex projection of all ~490 entities, with no visible
rays connecting the object to its projection, and the scene's actual radius-1000 ground sphere swept
a giant, confusing ring across the frame (its top touches world Y=0, right where everything else
sits). `PipelineStageRenderer.cpp`'s `renderProjectiveStage()` now instead: computes an axis-aligned
box over every non-excluded entity's real extent (`computeObjectBox()`) as a curated stand-in for
"the object" (the same reasoning `buildSceneWireframe()` already applies to spheres — a proxy
instead of every vertex, since sight lines to ~490 entities' worth of vertices would be a solid fan,
nothing like a legible diagram); draws one continuous sight-line ray per box corner from the eye to
that real 3D point (`kSightLineColor`, magenta); draws the same box's corners ray-cast through the
picture plane and reconnected with the box's own edges as the "deformed"/traced outline
(`kDeformedColor`); and draws a schematic reference-plane quad at the box's lowest Y instead of the
actual ground sphere, which `buildSceneWireframe()` now takes an `excludeOversizedEntities` flag to
skip drawing entirely in this stage (still dimmed-but-present for every other entity, so the diagram
reads as "sight lines + object overlaid on the real scene" rather than replacing it). The Camera-Matrix
stage got a matching small fix: `appendCameraGizmo()` draws a small wireframe camera-body pyramid at
the origin (pale yellow) alongside the existing coordinate-axis triad, so "here's the camera, facing
this way" reads as more than three colored lines pointing in different directions.

A follow-up user screenshot circled several long, near-parallel white lines shooting past the
frame's edge in the Projective-Matrix diagram. Root-caused numerically (a standalone harness
printing each object-box corner's ray/plane intersection parameter) rather than guessed: the object
box was built from *every* non-excluded entity regardless of whether the camera could actually see
it, but the field of small spheres spans a much wider area (~22 units) than the camera's ~20°
vertical FOV actually captures at this distance — so the axis-aligned box's *combinatorial* corners
(e.g. one sphere's extreme +X mixed with a different, distant sphere's extreme +Z, a synthetic point
no real entity occupies) could end up nearly edge-on to the camera, making `projectOntoPlane()`'s
ray/plane intersection denominator near zero and the projected point shoot off to an absurd distance
(one measured over 400 units away, against the scene's own ~20-unit scale). Fixed on two levels:
`computeObjectBox()` now excludes any entity outside the camera's actual field of view (with no
margin — confirmed numerically that even a modest 50% margin still let some corners diverge, since
the combinatorial-corner problem persists independent of how many individual entities qualify), and
`renderProjectiveStage()` additionally checks each of the 8 corners' ray/plane intersection parameter
and skips (rather than draws) any deformed-box edge touching a corner whose parameter falls outside
a sane range — a defensive backstop, not the primary fix, since combinatorial corners can't be fully
eliminated by entity-level filtering alone.

An optional "Pipeline Steps" secondary window (`App/PipelineVisualizationWindow.hpp/.cpp`, opened
via a new `Pipeline Steps` button in `ControlsPanel`) visualizes the four traditional-pipeline
coordinate spaces a scene passes through on the way to a rasterized image — user request, not
something the milestone plan called for. In this exact order: **Projective Matrix** (world space:
the camera's position, the image plane's position/orientation, and the scene "deformed" onto that
plane), **Camera Matrix** (the scene after the view transform — camera at `(0,0,0)` looking down
`-Z` with `+Y` up), **Orthographic Matrix** (after projection + perspective divide — an image with
X/Y in `[-1,1]`), and **Viewport Matrix** (the actual final rendered image). The fourth stage is
exactly the existing `RasterRenderer`'s own output — re-rendered through this window's *own*
`RasterRenderer` instance (not the main window's) specifically so a "Refresh" click here can never
race a `Render Raster`/`Compare` click on the main window over shared GPU resources. The first three
stages are new: `GPU/PipelineStageRenderer.hpp/.cpp` + `Shaders/Wireframe.metal` (a plain colored
line-segment shader, no lighting/depth-test, compiled from source at runtime like `Raster.metal`).

Drawing the scene for these diagrams reuses `Export/EntityMesh.hpp`'s `buildEntityMesh()` — but
**not** its full tessellated output: at ~490 entities that would be hundreds of thousands of line
segments, an unreadable tangle. Pyramids (only 5, already low-poly) get their exact wireframe;
spheres get a single 32-segment equatorial-ring proxy at their real center/radius instead (always
valid since spheres use the identity orientation basis — `Scene.cpp`'s `identityTransformAt()`).
This "world-space line-soup" is built once and reused, transformed differently, by each stage.

The camera math needed a genuine shared home rather than a second copy: `RasterRenderer.cpp`'s
`buildViewProjectionMatrix()` derived a view matrix from an orthonormal basis and a perspective
matrix from near/far + half-FOV tangents — exactly what the new stage-1/2 "external observer"
cameras also need (they look *at* the scene's own camera from outside, not through it, so they have
no `CameraGPU` of their own). Factored into a new `GPU/CameraMath.hpp` (`buildViewMatrix()`,
`buildPerspectiveProjection()`, and the scene-camera-specific `buildSceneViewProjectionMatrix()`,
plus the shared `kSceneNearPlane`/`kSceneFarPlane` constants) — `RasterRenderer.cpp` now calls these
instead of keeping its own copy, a pure refactor verified by the existing `RasterRendererTests`
passing unmodified. Stage 3 ("Orthographic Matrix") reuses `buildSceneViewProjectionMatrix()`
completely unchanged, so it's a faithful intermediate step of the exact same pipeline the raster
renderer already runs, not a lookalike approximation.

A framing pitfall was deliberately designed around rather than reproduced: the ground sphere alone
has radius 1000 against a ~22-unit field of radius-0.2 spheres (see the export-corruption paragraph
below for that exact figure) — this is the same scale disparity this session's Unity-import
investigation diagnosed as making a freshly-imported scene *look* like everything collapsed to one
point once a camera tries to frame the whole thing. The stage-1/2 "external observer" cameras that
auto-frame themselves to see the scene (`collectFramingPoints()`/`fitFramingSphere()`) explicitly
exclude any entity whose own bounding radius exceeds a constant threshold (50 units) from that
framing fit — the ground sphere is still drawn (as a giant ring sweeping through the diagram, since
it's genuinely part of the scene), it just doesn't get to decide how zoomed-out the camera is. This
lesson is encoded as a permanent regression test
(`RayTracerBenchTests/PipelineStageTests.cpp`'s `collectFramingPoints_excludesOversizedEntities_...`),
not just a one-off fix.

Stage 1's "objects deformed so they can be painted into the image plane" visual is a literal

camera's origin through the image plane (`CameraGPU`'s own `lowerLeftCorner`/`horizontal`/`vertical`

the two facts that must hold for this to be correct (the 4 plane corners are fixed points of their
own projection; a point on the camera's forward axis lands at the plane's center), not just eyeballed.
Vertices behind the camera aren't clipped — an explicit, documented scope limit, since this app's

`ThirdParty/metal-cpp-extensions/AppKit/NSWindow.hpp` needed one more small addition:
`setReleasedWhenClosed(bool)`. Cocoa's default for a programmatically-created `NSWindow` is `YES`,
meaning `close()` — including via the user clicking the window's own title-bar close button —
deallocates the underlying object; without this, `AppDelegate`'s lazily-created
`PipelineVisualizationWindow*` pointer would dangle after the user closed it once, crashing on the
next "Pipeline Steps" click. Same `Object::sendMessage` idiom this file's existing

A third render path was added alongside the CPU and GPU ray tracers: `GPU/RasterRenderer.hpp/.cpp` +
`Shaders/Raster.metal` render the identical `SceneDescription` through Metal's *standard* graphics
pipeline (`MTL::RenderPipelineState`, a vertex/fragment shader pair, a real depth buffer) instead of
a compute kernel — the "normal" way GPUs draw 3D scenes, wired into `ControlsPanel`'s new "Render
Raster" button and folded into `Compare` (which now runs all three renderers) per explicit user
request, not something the milestone plan called for. Its geometry is `Export/EntityMesh.hpp`'s
`buildEntityMesh()` output — the exact same world-space triangle mesh the glTF/OBJ exporter already
builds per entity — flattened across every entity into one combined vertex/index buffer (a flat
per-entity display color folded in from `MaterialGPU`, since the flat-Lambertian shading below has
no use for glTF/OBJ's fuller PBR/MTL field sets) and drawn in a single `drawIndexedPrimitives` call
per frame; rebuilt on every `render()` call exactly like `GPURenderer::rebuildBuffers()` already
does, since this is an on-demand benchmark click, not a hot loop. The camera is derived, not
duplicated: rather than adding new shared camera fields to `ShaderTypes.h`, `buildViewProjectionMatrix()`
recovers an exact view+perspective-projection matrix from `CameraGPU`'s *existing* precomputed
fields — `u`/`v`/`w` are already exactly a right-handed right/up/backward basis (matching Metal's
-Z-forward view space, since `Scene.cpp`'s `makeCamera()` defines `w = normalize(lookFrom-lookAt)`),
and the focus distance and both half-FOV tangents fall out algebraically from the precomputed
viewport corner/extent vectors (`focusDist = dot(origin - viewportCenter, w)`, then each half-FOV
tangent is that axis's viewport extent over `2*focusDist`) — so the raster and ray-traced paths are
structurally incapable of using two different cameras for "the same scene." Near/far planes
(`0.05`/`5000`) are fixed constants sized off the scene's known extent (the ground sphere alone spans
±1000 units — see the export-bug paragraph below for that exact figure), chosen and then confirmed,
not just assumed, by an offscreen render that visibly shows the whole scene rather than a clipped
piece of it. Shading is a deliberately basic, unlit-ish model — one fixed directional light plus a
flat ambient term, no shadows/reflections/refraction/GI — documented as the intended point of a
raster-vs-ray-tracer comparison rather than a gap to close, the same way `MAT_DIELECTRIC`'s glTF/OBJ
approximation is already documented as an approximation rather than physical equivalence. Back-face
culling is deliberately left off (`MTL::CullModeNone`, Metal's default): `EntityMesh.cpp`'s winding
order was only ever verified against "the triangle's normal points away from the shape's interior"
(`RayTracerBenchTests/EntityMeshTests.cpp`), never against a specific front-face handedness
convention, so culling risked silently dropping correct triangles for negligible benefit at this
scene's size. `ResultsPanel` gained a fourth line (triangle count instead of rays/sec — rasterization
has no "rays" to count) and `AppDelegate::updateSpeedupIfPossible()` was generalized from one hardcoded
CPU-vs-GPU ratio to every pairwise ratio among whichever of CPU/GPU/Raster are currently known, so it
still shows exactly today's single-pair message until Raster has actually been run once. The
magnifier loupe (see below) was extended to sync across all three previews for the same reason it
existed for two. Verified end-to-end: `RayTracerBenchTests/RasterRendererTests.cpp` (2 new tests,
against the real `RasterRenderer`, not a reimplementation) checks the output texture's size and that
it isn't blank/uniform, and that its triangle count matches an independent sum over
`buildEntityMesh()` — deliberately not a pixel-parity check against the ray tracer, since a
rasterized image is *expected* to look different (no shadows/GI/reflection); a standalone offscreen
harness rendering the real ~490-entity default scene and writing the result to a PNG confirmed by
eye that the raster output shows the correct scene — recognizable ground plane, feature spheres,
randomized field, and oriented pyramids, all in the right positions and occluding each other
correctly — not just "some non-blank pixels." All 24 tests (22 previous + 2 new) pass through both a
plain CMake/Makefiles build and the real `xcodebuild` build of the regenerated `.xcodeproj`.

`exportSceneAsGLTF()` had a real, silent-corruption bug: `appendVec3s()` bulk-`memcpy`'d `v.size() * sizeof(float) * 3` (12) bytes straight out of a `std::vector<simd_float3>`, but `simd_float3` is a SIMD-register type — `sizeof`/`alignof` 16, not 12, a padded 4th lane confirmed via a standalone `sizeof`/stride check rather than assumed — so consecutive elements are 16 bytes apart in memory, not 12. The bulk copy therefore read across the wrong element boundaries and scrambled every vertex past the first one in each mesh. Caught by decoding a real exported `.gltf`'s base64 buffer byte-for-byte and diffing it against the source `MeshData` rather than by inspection: the ground sphere's true bounds are `(-1000,-2000,-1000)…(1000,0,1000)`, but the corrupted buffer decoded to `(-2000,-2000,-2000)…(1000,1000,924)` — components shuffled between vertices, not merely off by noise. `exportSceneAsOBJ()` was unaffected (it writes each `float` field individually via `operator<<`, never a bulk `memcpy`), which is exactly why the two formats' bounding boxes disagreed and gave the bug away. Fixed by copying each vertex's 3 floats individually, skipping the padding lane. While in there, also fixed a second, smaller correctness gap the same decode-and-compare check surfaced: the glTF accessor `min`/`max` bounds were serialized at the stream's default 6-significant-digit precision, which routinely rounds to a value numerically *inside* the real data range — invalid per the glTF spec ("declared min/max must bound the actual data"), confirmed by finding the declared bound was too tight on 1469 of 2946 checked components. `max_digits10` for `float` (9 digits) turned out not to be quite enough either, because glTF numbers are parsed back as `double`, not `float`, and a 9-digit string that round-trips correctly *as a float* can still land a hair off the true widened-to-double value; `max_digits10` for `double` (17 digits) closed the gap completely (0 invalid components afterward). The OBJ/MTL writer's default 6-digit precision was bumped to 9 (float-exact, no double-vs-float subtlety there) for the same reason: a subsequent load's fidelity depends on it. All of this was verified the same way this project already verifies its exports (see below) — `assimp info` and Python's `trimesh`/`json`/`struct` on the real regenerated files — plus it's now a permanent regression test (`RayTracerBenchTests/SceneImportExportTests.cpp`), not just an ad hoc harness.

A new `Export/SceneImporter.hpp/.cpp` (added to the framework-free `RayTracerCore` library, alongside `SceneExporter`) reverses `SceneExporter.hpp`: `importSceneFromGLTF()`/`importSceneFromOBJ()` reconstruct a scene's entities/materials from a `.gltf`/`.obj` this app itself previously wrote, wired to a new "Load Scene" button in `ControlsPanel` (row 1, since row 2 was already packed — see `AppDelegate::loadScene()`). This is deliberately a *round-trip* reader for this app's own exports, not a general-purpose glTF/OBJ importer: it exploits the fact that `buildEntityMesh()` only ever emits one of two recognizable shapes — a 117-vertex shared-vertex UV sphere (`(kSphereLatSegments+1)*(kSphereLonSegments+1)`, now exposed from `EntityMesh.hpp` so the importer can't silently drift out of sync with the exporter's tessellation density) or an 18-vertex unshared-vertex faceted pyramid — and reconstructs each exactly (to the file's own float-text precision) from that mesh: a sphere's bounding box touches center±radius exactly on every axis (the tessellation always samples the axis-aligned extremes), and a pyramid's fixed, known vertex-emission order (4 side triangles ending in the apex, then 2 base triangles) yields a closed-form inverse for position/right/up/forward/baseHalfWidth/height. Materials are reconstructed by reversing `gltfMaterialFor()`/`mtlFieldsFor()`; the one genuinely lossy case (`MAT_DIELECTRIC`'s index of refraction/exact albedo aren't encoded by either format) is a no-op in practice because `buildDefaultScene()` always constructs dielectrics with `ir=1.5`/`albedo=(0,0,0)`, which is exactly what the importer defaults to. A mesh matching neither vertex-count signature is reported as a skipped/unrecognized entity rather than silently guessed at. Camera/render params were never part of either export format (geometry only), so the importer borrows them from a throwaway `buildDefaultScene()` call at the caller's current settings rather than hand-duplicating `Scene.cpp`'s private `makeCamera()`; `AppDelegate::sceneForRender()` does the analogous thing for a *loaded* scene on every subsequent render — geometry from `_loadedScene`, camera/params refreshed from whatever the controls currently say — so width/samples-per-pixel/max-depth edits keep taking effect after a load exactly like they already do on a generated scene. Once a scene is loaded, Render CPU/GPU/Compare *and* Save glTF/OBJ all operate on it (not a freshly regenerated one) until another Load Scene replaces it; there's deliberately no separate "unload" control — going back to a generated scene means loading a different file, since that wasn't asked for and would have doubled the state-machine surface for a fairly narrow benefit.

The glTF path needed a minimal hand-rolled JSON parser (objects/arrays/strings/numbers/bool/null; no external dependency, matching this project's "roll our own small helper" stance already used for base64) and a base64 *decoder* — the existing base64 *encoder* was a private helper inside `SceneExporter.cpp`, so both now live in a shared `Export/Base64.hpp/.cpp` (encode moved there unchanged; decode is new) rather than duplicating the alphabet table in two places where it could drift. `metal-cpp-extensions` had no `NSOpenPanel` wrapper at all — the same confirmed, permanent gap as `NSButton`/`NSControl`/`NSTextField`/`NSAlert` — filled at `ThirdParty/metal-cpp-extensions/AppKit/NSOpenPanel.hpp` using the identical `Object::sendMessage` idiom, deliberately minimal (just `openPanel()`/`setCanChooseFiles()`/`setCanChooseDirectories()`/`setAllowsMultipleSelection()`/`runModal()`/`url()` — no `allowedContentTypes`/`allowedFileTypes` filter wrapper; the app tells `.gltf` from `.obj` apart by the chosen file's own extension after the fact instead). Verified end-to-end with a standalone harness exercising the real production code (not a reimplementation): exported the real ~490-entity default scene, decoded the actual written bytes to confirm the corruption fix and the min/max fix independently, round-tripped it through both importers and diffed every entity's reconstructed Transform/Shape/Material against the original (worst-case drift ~1e-6, at float32 text-precision noise, not a structural error), and rendered both the original and reconstructed scenes through the real `renderCPU()` at a fixed seed — 99.93% of output bytes were bit-identical, with the small remainder isolated to the same "sub-ULP float difference flips a chaotic Monte Carlo scatter branch" phenomenon this file's Verification approach section already documents for CPU/GPU parity, not a new failure mode.

Also beyond the plan doc's scope: a "Floating?" checkbox in `ControlsPanel` — a real `NSButtonTypeSwitch` checkbox (`NS::Button::setButtonType`/`setState`/`state`, added to `NSButton.hpp`), not the CPU-threading toggle's "push button whose title changes" hack, since AppKit manages a switch button's checked state itself and `currentSettings()` just reads `state()` on demand. When checked, `Scene::buildDefaultScene()`'s new `floating` parameter (default `false`, so existing callers/tests are unaffected) places the small randomized-field spheres at a random height in `[radius, kMaxFloatHeight]` instead of resting on the ground — the three large feature spheres (glass/lambertian/metal) always stay on the ground regardless of `floating`, per explicit user feedback after the first pass floated everything. `kMaxFloatHeight = 3.0f` was chosen by actually rendering the fixed camera setup at that height and confirming by eye that nothing floats out of frame, on both the CPU and GPU renderer, not derived from an unverified frustum formula (an exact per-object analytical bound was worked out by hand first but not trusted without a live render to check it against, so the simpler, directly-verifiable global constant was used instead).

Both `ImageDisplayView`s support a magnifying-glass loupe — hovering the mouse over either preview zooms the same normalized UV position in *both* previews simultaneously (the point being to compare CPU vs. GPU detail at high zoom, not just magnify one image in isolation). Implemented as a lens effect baked into `Shaders/Blit.metal`'s fragment shader (samples a de-magnified region within a circular, aspect-corrected radius around a lens center, with a thin border ring), driven by mouse tracking via a local `NS::EventMaskMouseMoved` monitor (`NS::Event::addLocalMonitorForEventsMatchingMask`, a block-based API — Objective-C blocks work fine as `sendMessage` arguments in plain C++, same as `MTL::Device::newBuffer`'s deallocator parameter already vendored elsewhere) and `NS::View::convertPoint(point, fromView)` for window-to-view-local coordinate conversion (both were missing from metal-cpp-extensions, added the same way as every other gap below). Verified three ways rather than assumed: an offscreen shader test with a known 2px marker confirmed exact 4x magnification with no positional drift; a coordinate-math test using production-matching window/view frames confirmed `convertPoint` plus the AppKit-Y-up-to-texture-V-down flip (`v = 1.0 - localY/height`) map correctly; and an end-to-end test through the real scene/CPURenderer/Blit.metal pipeline confirmed the lens zooms into actual, recognizable, correctly-positioned scene detail.

Also beyond the plan doc's scope: the scene's geometry is organized ECS-style rather than as one sphere-only array. `ShaderTypes.h` defines `TransformGPU` (position + an orthonormal right/up/forward orientation basis) and `ShapeGPU` (a tagged union of per-primitive dimensions plus a `materialIndex` component reference) as the Transform and Shape components; `SceneDescription` (`Scene.hpp`) stores them as parallel `transforms`/`shapes` arrays indexed by a plain entity index, alongside the pre-existing `materials` component array — no `SphereGPU`-only representation remains. `RayTraceCore.h`'s `hitEntity()` is the "collision system": it dispatches on `ShapeGPU::type` to `hitSphere()` or `hitPyramid()`, the direct ECS analogue of `scatter()`'s tagged-switch material dispatch, and the replacement for a virtual `Hittable::hit()` call (forbidden in MSL kernels). `rayColor()`'s scene loop walks `transforms[]`/`shapes[]` in lockstep and calls `hitEntity()` per entity — adding a third primitive means one more `SHAPE_*` case, not a new subclass.

Square pyramids were added as the second primitive, in a spread of 3D orientations (`Scene.cpp`'s `buildDefaultScene()`, after the three feature spheres) — deliberately the shape that exercises `TransformGPU`'s orientation basis at all, since a sphere looks identical under any rotation. `hitPyramidLocal()` represents a square pyramid as the intersection of 5 half-spaces (1 base + 4 triangular sides) and solves it with the Kay-Kajiya slab method generalized from an axis-aligned box's 6 planes to these 5 — each side face's plane equation falls out of the pyramid's symmetry in closed form (e.g. the +X face is `height·x + baseHalfWidth·y = baseHalfWidth·height`), so no per-face numerical fitting was needed. `hitPyramid()` transforms the incoming ray into the pyramid's local space by projecting onto its orthonormal basis (the inverse of an orthogonal matrix is just its transpose, so projecting onto each basis vector *is* the inverse rotation — no matrix inverse needed) and maps the hit normal back the same way. Pyramids are always lambertian or metal, never dielectric: the slab test only resolves the ray's entry face, so a ray originating inside the solid (as glass refraction requires once a ray exits the far side) isn't handled — an explicit, documented scope limit, not an oversight. Each pyramid's ground-resting position is computed exactly (`makeRestingPyramidTransform()`): unlike `kMaxFloatHeight` below (which needed a rendered check against the camera frustum because no closed form was trusted), the lowest of a pyramid's 5 local vertices under a given orientation *is* solvable in closed form, so every pyramid sits flush on the ground at any tilt with no per-orientation eyeball check. Verified by an offscreen CPU render and a separate offscreen GPU render of the identical scene/seed, confirming both produce the same pyramid silhouettes, orientations, and materials pixel-for-pixel (beyond the existing `DeterministicParityTests`, which continued passing unmodified once the entity-array refactor above was in place, confirming the ECS restructuring didn't change either renderer's actual output).

A new `Export/` module (`EntityMesh.hpp/.cpp`, `SceneExporter.hpp/.cpp`, added to the framework-free `RayTracerCore` static library) lets `ControlsPanel`'s new "Save glTF"/"Save OBJ" buttons write the *current* scene's geometry (not a rendered image — glTF/OBJ are 3D scene formats) to `<the running executable's own directory>/SavedScenes/`. `buildEntityMesh()` is a third ECS "system" alongside `hitEntity()`'s ray intersection and `scatter()`'s shading — same Transform+Shape dispatch, a different operation over the same components: spheres are tessellated into an 8×12 UV grid (no native sphere primitive in either format; skipping the zero-area triangle each pole row would otherwise emit, rather than emitting degenerate geometry), while pyramids are exact, faceted (unshared-vertex, flat-normal) meshes built from the same 5 local vertices `hitPyramidLocal()`'s planes are derived from. Both mesh generators' winding orders were derived by hand and then verified programmatically (`RayTracerBenchTests/EntityMeshTests.cpp` checks every triangle's cross-product normal points away from the shape's interior) rather than trusted by inspection — an initial sphere-winding attempt was in fact backwards and caught this way. `executableDirectory()` resolves the running binary's real location via `_NSGetExecutablePath` (not `argv[0]` or `getcwd()`, since this app can be launched from anywhere), and `sceneFilenameStem()` encodes the seed/width/Floating? state plus a timestamp — deliberately *not* samplesPerPixel/maxDepth, which affect rendering but not the exported geometry. Materials map onto each format's native model (glTF PBR metallic-roughness; OBJ/MTL's Kd/Ks/Ns/d/illum) with dielectric approximated the same way in both (a clear, glossy, partly-transparent surface — neither format's core spec has a real glass/transmission model, and `KHR_materials_transmission` was deliberately skipped to keep the glTF a plain, universally-loadable file), documented as an approximation rather than presented as physically equivalent to the raytraced result. Verified end-to-end rather than assumed: a standalone harness exported the real ~490-entity default scene and both outputs were validated with `assimp info` and Python's `trimesh` (matching triangle counts between formats, correct material colors/counts, well-formed glTF JSON), and a temporary env-var-gated hook (added, run through the actual built app, then reverted — same technique as this project's other headless-session verifications) confirmed the real button-click path writes files next to the real running binary using the real current control settings.

Each 3D export is followed by a same-named `.png` preview, written to the identical `SavedScenes/` directory — a real render at the current controls settings (the same image a "Render CPU" click would produce for that seed), not a placeholder or thumbnail, via a new `Export/ImageWriter.hpp/.cpp` wrapping CoreGraphics/ImageIO's plain C `CGImageCreate`/`CGImageDestination*` APIs (real system frameworks, no vendored image library, no Objective-C — the same "C API, no `.mm` shim needed" approach this project already uses for Metal/QuartzCore). `ImageWriter.cpp` is only linked into the `RayTracerBench` app target, not the framework-free `RayTracerCore` library, since it needs those two frameworks. Adding a full CPU render meant `AppDelegate::saveScene()` could no longer stay a fast, synchronous, main-thread-only operation (per its prior design note below) — it now runs on a background `std::thread` exactly like `startCPURender`/`startGPURender`/`startCompare`, marshaling the result alert back via `dispatch_async`. Verified with the same temporary env-gated hook technique as the 3D export above: run through the real built app (using `pkill` rather than an in-process auto-terminate, since the save flow is now asynchronous and has no natural "done" signal to hook), confirming a real `.gltf`/`.obj`+`.mtl` and a real, correctly-rendered `.png` (spot-checked visually) land side by side with the exact same stem.

`ControlsPanel`/`ResultsPanel` needed one more AppKit gap filled the same way as `NS::Button`/`CA::MetalLayer`: metal-cpp-extensions has no `NS::TextField` at all, added at `ThirdParty/metal-cpp-extensions/AppKit/NSTextField.hpp` (used both as an editable numeric input and, with `setEditable(false)`/`setBezeled(false)`/`setDrawsBackground(false)`, as a plain label — one class covers both roles). `NS::Control` also gained `setEnabled()`/`isEnabled()`, used to disable every field/button while a render is in flight (prevents a second click from racing the shared `GPURenderer`/`ImageDisplayView` state — settings are read via `currentSettings()` synchronously on the main thread *before* the background thread starts, so there's no window for a second click to slip in).

Verifying "doesn't freeze the UI" hit the same environment limitation as screenshotting earlier: `osascript`/System Events has no assistive-access permission in this background session (`-25211`, confirmed explicitly, not assumed), so UI automation isn't available here. The fix was the same kind of workaround as the offscreen GPU readback test: add a temporary, env-var-gated self-test hook (a main-thread heartbeat + an auto-triggered `Compare`) directly in the app, observe it, then revert it — don't leave ad hoc test hooks committed, and don't skip verifying a claim just because the obvious tool (`screencapture`/`osascript`) is unavailable in this environment.

`Shaders/Raytracer.metal` `#include`s `Core/ShaderTypes.h` and `Core/RayTraceCore.h` verbatim, which means it **cannot** be compiled from an in-memory source string the way `Shaders/Blit.metal` is (no file location for the relative `#include`s to resolve against — this failed loudly and immediately at runtime the first time it was tried). Instead, `CMakeLists.txt` has an `add_custom_command` that shells out to `xcrun -sdk macosx metal`/`metallib` at build time to produce a real `.metallib`, whose path is injected as the `RT_RAYTRACER_METALLIB` compile definition; `GPURenderer` loads it via `newLibrary(filepath, &error)`, not `newLibrary(sourceString, ...)`. Before wiring `GPURenderer` at all, `RayTraceCore.h` was validated standalone against the real Metal compiler (`xcrun -sdk macosx metal -c ... -Wall -Wextra`) — it compiled clean on the first attempt, zero warnings, confirming the value-returning design described below actually holds up under MSL and not just in theory.

`App/ImageDisplayView.hpp/.cpp` is a `CAMetalLayer`-backed `NS::View` (not `NSImageView`, not `MTKView` — per this project's UI architecture) that blits a source `MTL::Texture` onto screen via `Shaders/Blit.metal`, a tiny vertex+fragment textured-quad shader compiled from source at runtime (`newLibrary(sourceString, ...)`, not `newDefaultLibrary()`) since this project is deliberately a plain executable, not an app bundle with a compiled `default.metallib` resource — the shader source is read from disk at the path CMake injects as the `RT_SHADERS_DIR` compile definition. `updatePixels()` re-creates the source texture only when width/height change, uploads via `replaceRegion`, and renders+presents immediately (no continuous draw loop — this is an on-demand benchmark preview, not an animation). Verified correct with an offscreen GPU test (four-quadrant test texture through the actual shader, read back via `getBytes`) rather than a screenshot, since screenshots require the physical display to be unlocked, which cannot be relied on in a background session — prefer that kind of readback-based verification over `screencapture` for future GPU/display work here unless you've confirmed the screen is actually unlocked first.

Both `NS::View` and metal-cpp itself needed project-local fixes/additions to make `ImageDisplayView` possible: `NS::View::init()` as Apple shipped it sent `initWithFrame:` to the **class** object instead of an allocated instance (verified via a real crash before fixing) — now fixed to `alloc()`+`init()` like `NS::Window`; `addSubview()`/`setWantsLayer()`/`setLayer()` were added to the same file (missing entirely, same class of gap as the `NS::Button` situation below). `CA::MetalLayer` doesn't exist anywhere in vendored metal-cpp either (only forward-declared as the return type of `CA::MetalDrawable::layer()`) — filled in at `ThirdParty/metal-cpp-extensions/QuartzCore/CAMetalLayer.hpp`, same idiom as the AppKit additions. Metal/QuartzCore's private-implementation macros (`MTL_PRIVATE_IMPLEMENTATION`, `CA_PRIVATE_IMPLEMENTATION`) are defined in `ImageDisplayView.cpp`, not `main.cpp` — each such macro must be defined in exactly the one `.cpp` that first includes the corresponding header, or you get undefined `Private::Class`/`Private::Selector` symbols at link time (`main.cpp` keeps only `NS_PRIVATE_IMPLEMENTATION`, since it's the one that includes `AppKit/AppKit.hpp`).

`RayTraceCore.h`'s dual-compile design keeps every function passing/returning plain values (RNG state, hit/scatter outcomes, camera-ray samples all thread through return-value structs like `RandomFloatSample`/`HitResult`/`ScatterResult`, never out-parameters) specifically so only one address-space keyword (`device`, via the `RT_DEVICE` macro) is ever needed — on `rayColor()`'s two scene-array pointers, the only place a `device`-space buffer pointer is touched directly. Follow that same pattern (value in, value+state out) when extending this file, rather than reaching for reference/pointer out-parameters, which would need a second, MSL-only address-space keyword (`thread`) that plain C++ doesn't have a use for. Also note `simd_float3(x, y, z)` function-call construction does not compile in plain C++ (only brace-init or `simd_make_float3` do) even though MSL's `float3(x, y, z)` is fine — use the `makeFloat3()` helper already defined in this file for any new 3-arg vector literals shared between the two compilers; `Scene.cpp` is pure host C++ (never Metal-compiled) and uses the plain `simd_make_float3`/`simd_normalize`/`simd_cross`/`simd_length` C API directly instead.

`RayTracerBench.xcodeproj` is generated via CMake's Xcode generator rather than Xcode's GUI project templates, since project creation here happens headlessly. `CMakeLists.txt` is the source of truth for target/framework/include-path setup — regenerate the `.xcodeproj` from it after changing sources rather than hand-editing the project file, and re-copy over the tracked copy: `cmake -S . -B .cmake-xcode -G Xcode`, then `cp .cmake-xcode/RayTracerBench.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj RayTracerBench.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj` (only the `project.pbxproj` file — `xcuserdata` inside the tracked `.xcodeproj` is local state, not part of the generated output, and shouldn't be touched).

**`-B .cmake-xcode` is not a suggestion — it must always be that exact, persistent, gitignored directory** (never a one-off `/tmp` scratch path, and never deleted once a project has been generated against it without immediately regenerating + re-copying). Confirmed the hard way: CMake's Xcode generator bakes several absolute paths as literal strings — not `$(...)` build-setting references Xcode could re-resolve later — directly from `CMAKE_BINARY_DIR` (the `-B` directory) at *generation* time, and none of them go through `SYMROOT` at all: inter-target link references (e.g. `RayTracerBench` linking `RayTracerCore` gets `<CMAKE_BINARY_DIR>/Debug/libRayTracerCore.a` written straight into `OTHER_LDFLAGS`, not a proper "Link Binary With Libraries" product reference), the `Raytracer.metallib` custom-command paths, and CMake's own `ZERO_CHECK`/`ReRunCMake.make` bookkeeping. A `CMAKE_XCODE_ATTRIBUTE_SYMROOT` override was tried as a fix and found to be a dead end on two counts: it doesn't affect any of the above (they're generated independently of `SYMROOT`), and forcing `CONFIGURATION_BUILD_DIR` to track `SYMROOT` via a `$(SYMROOT)/$(CONFIGURATION)` variable reference moves *only* where a target's own product gets copied — not where a dependent target's hardcoded `OTHER_LDFLAGS` entry looks for it, which breaks the link instead. So there's deliberately no `SYMROOT` override at all now: products build under `.cmake-xcode/<Configuration>/` (e.g. `.cmake-xcode/Debug/RayTracerBenchTests` — the "Test" command below already resolves this dynamically via `-showBuildSettings` rather than hardcoding it), Xcode's default `SYMROOT` (`.cmake-xcode/build`, harmlessly unused/empty) is what `Product > Clean Build Folder`/`xcodebuild clean` deletes, and CMake's own cache/scripts one level up survive a Clean untouched. If `xcodebuild clean` ever again fails with "Could not delete `<dir>` because it was not created by the build system," that means something (a plain `cmake --build`, or copying/generating by hand) wrote directly into that `SYMROOT` directory outside of Xcode's own bookkeeping — the fix Xcode itself prints (`xattr -w com.apple.xcode.CreatedByBuildSystem true <dir>`) is the right one, not disabling or routing around Clean.

`ThirdParty/metal-cpp` and `ThirdParty/metal-cpp-extensions` are vendored from Apple's `LearnMetalCPP.zip` sample bundle (not the standalone `metal-cpp` zip) to keep the base library and AppKit/MetalKit extensions from Apple's own release pairing in sync. Apple's `metal-cpp-extensions` has no `NS::Control`/`NS::Button` wrapper — this is a confirmed, permanent gap (an Apple DTS engineer said so on the developer forums), not an oversight to work around differently. `ThirdParty/metal-cpp-extensions/AppKit/NSControl.hpp` and `NSButton.hpp` are project-local additions (clearly marked as such in their headers) that fill it using the exact same `Object::sendMessage`/`class_addMethod` idiom Apple's own headers use — extend that pattern for any other missing AppKit control rather than reaching for an `.mm` shim first. The same pattern has since covered `NSTextField.hpp` (used both as an editable input and, with `setEditable(false)`/`setBezeled(false)`/`setDrawsBackground(false)`, as a label), `NSAlert.hpp` (for `AboutAlert`), `QuartzCore/CAMetalLayer.hpp` (for `ImageDisplayView`), and `NSEvent.hpp` + `NS::View::convertPoint()` (for the magnifier's mouse tracking) — none of these exist in vendored metal-cpp-extensions either. Every one of these additions was verified with a real standalone compile+run before being built on top of, not just written and assumed correct.

## What this project is

RayTracerBench: a native macOS app (Xcode) that renders the same scene with both a CPU ray tracer and a GPU (Metal compute) ray tracer and compares their performance. It's based on the algorithm/structure of "Ray Tracing in One Weekend" (CC0) and its CUDA port by Roger Allen (public domain) — structural precedent only, no copied source.

Written in **pure C++ and Metal** via Apple's header-only `metal-cpp` + `metal-cpp-extensions` bindings, deliberately avoiding SwiftUI/Objective-C++. The goal is zero hand-written `.mm` files; if a specific AppKit widget's callback wiring proves unworkable through `metal-cpp-extensions`, the documented fallback (Plan B, not default) is one small isolated `.mm` shim for just that widget.

## Build and test commands

- Build: Xcode GUI (`Product > Run`), or `xcodebuild -project RayTracerBench.xcodeproj -scheme RayTracerBench -configuration Debug build`
- Test: `xcodebuild -project RayTracerBench.xcodeproj -scheme RayTracerBenchTests -configuration Debug build`, then run the built binary directly (find it via `xcodebuild -showBuildSettings -scheme RayTracerBenchTests | grep TARGET_BUILD_DIR`, or just run it from Xcode's GUI). **`xcodebuild test -scheme RayTracerBenchTests` does not work** — confirmed by trying it, not assumed — since `RayTracerBenchTests` is a plain command-line C++ tool (no XCTest/ObjC), so its CMake-generated scheme has no Test action configured; `xcodebuild test` fails with "Scheme ... is not currently configured for the test action."

## Architecture

### The core correctness constraint

Metal Shading Language forbids virtual functions, RTTI, and dynamic allocation in kernel code. Unlike the CUDA port (which keeps C++ virtual dispatch for `hittable`/`material`), both the GPU **and** CPU renderers here use tagged structs + switch-based dispatch over flat arrays — the same style — so the CPU/GPU comparison is apples-to-apples. This is achieved by having both renderers execute the *same shared algorithm source*, not two independently-written implementations that merely look similar.

### Shared core (`Core/`)

Geometry is organized ECS-style (see "Project status" above for the rationale): entities are plain array indices into `SceneDescription`'s parallel `transforms`/`shapes` component arrays, not a polymorphic shape hierarchy.

- `ShaderTypes.h` — plain structs shared byte-for-byte between C++ and MSL via `<simd/simd.h>` types (no hand-rolled vec3): `MaterialGPU` (tagged enum: `MAT_LAMBERTIAN`/`MAT_METAL`/`MAT_DIELECTRIC`), `TransformGPU` (the Transform component: `position` + an orthonormal `right`/`up`/`forward` orientation basis), `ShapeGPU` (the Shape component: tagged enum `SHAPE_SPHERE`/`SHAPE_PYRAMID`, per-primitive dimensions, and `materialIndex`), `CameraGPU`, `RenderParams`.
- `RayTraceCore.h` — **dual-compiled**: `#include`d verbatim by both `Shaders/Raytracer.metal` and `CPU/CPURenderer.cpp`. Contains `hitSphere()`, `hitPyramidLocal()`/`hitPyramid()` (square-pyramid intersection via a 5-plane slab test — see "Project status"), `hitEntity()` (the tagged-dispatch "collision system" over Transform+Shape components), tagged-switch `scatter()`, bounded-loop `rayColor()` (iterative, max depth ~50 — no recursion, so CPU and GPU stay structurally identical), `getRay()`, and a per-thread `pcgHash`/`randomFloat` RNG (replaces `curand`, used identically on both sides). A single `#ifdef __METAL_VERSION__` macro shim handles the one address-space-keyword difference between C++ and MSL. If shared compilation proves too fussy, the fallback is hand-duplicating the core functions with `// KEEP IN SYNC` comments at both ends — treat any divergence between the CPU and GPU copies as a bug.
- `Scene.hpp/.cpp` — `buildDefaultScene(seed, width, aspectRatio, spp, maxDepth, floating=false)` builds the classic RTIOW demo scene once (via seeded `std::mt19937`) into a single `SceneDescription` that both renderers consume — there is no separate CPU/GPU scene representation. Entities are spawned via `addEntity()`, pushing a `TransformGPU`/`ShapeGPU` pair onto the parallel component arrays. `floating` places the small randomized-field spheres at a random height up to `kMaxFloatHeight` instead of resting on the ground — the three large feature spheres and the square pyramids always stay grounded; only consumes extra RNG draws when `true`, so the default-`false` scene layout for a given seed is byte-for-byte unchanged from before this parameter existed.
- `Classic/` is an optional stretch goal only: a literal, polymorphic, book-verbatim port, explicitly excluded from timed comparisons since it doesn't share the tagged-struct code path with the GPU renderer.

### Scene export (`Export/`)

Framework-free C++17 (part of the `RayTracerCore` static library, like `Core/`), so it has no dependency on the GPU/App layers and needs no Metal/AppKit to run.

- `EntityMesh.hpp/.cpp` — `buildEntityMesh(transform, shape)` is a third ECS "system" (alongside `hitEntity()`'s intersection and `scatter()`'s shading): a tagged switch over `ShapeGPU::type` that builds a world-space triangle mesh instead of testing a ray. Spheres are tessellated (8×12 UV grid — no native sphere primitive in glTF/OBJ) with smooth per-vertex outward normals, skipping the zero-area triangle each pole row would otherwise emit. Pyramids are exact, faceted meshes (unshared vertices, flat per-triangle normals) built from the same 5 local vertices `RayTraceCore.h`'s `hitPyramidLocal()` derives its half-space planes from. Winding orders were hand-derived, then verified programmatically in `RayTracerBenchTests/EntityMeshTests.cpp` rather than trusted by inspection.
- `SceneExporter.hpp/.cpp` — `executableDirectory()` (via `_NSGetExecutablePath`) and `sceneFilenameStem()` (seed/width/Floating? + a timestamp — deliberately not spp/maxDepth, which don't affect the exported geometry) support `exportSceneAsGLTF()` and `exportSceneAsOBJ()`, which write into `<executableDirectory()>/SavedScenes/`. glTF is written as a single self-contained file (binary mesh data embedded as a base64 `data:` URI buffer, no companion `.bin`); OBJ is written alongside a companion `.mtl`. Vertex/accessor-bound floats are written at `max_digits10` precision (17 for the glTF accessor min/max, 9 for OBJ/MTL) rather than the stream default — see "Project status" for why that matters (a real corruption bug, plus a real glTF-spec-validity bug, both lived here). Material mapping approximates `MAT_DIELECTRIC` the same way in both formats (a clear, glossy, partly-transparent surface — neither format's core spec models real glass, and `KHR_materials_transmission` was deliberately skipped to keep the glTF plain and universally loadable). `ensureSavedScenesDirectoryPath()` is exposed publicly so `AppDelegate` can place the companion preview PNG (below) in the exact same directory without duplicating the resolution/creation logic.
- `SceneImporter.hpp/.cpp` — the inverse of `SceneExporter.hpp`: `importSceneFromGLTF()`/`importSceneFromOBJ()` reconstruct a `SceneDescription`'s entities/materials from a `.gltf`/`.obj` this app itself previously exported (a round-trip reader for this app's own files, not a general-purpose importer — see "Project status" for the full reconstruction approach and its scope limits). Contains a small hand-rolled JSON parser (glTF only) and uses `Base64.hpp`'s decoder.
- `Base64.hpp/.cpp` — RFC 4648 `base64Encode()`/`base64Decode()`, shared between `SceneExporter.cpp` (encode, for the glTF buffer's `data:` URI) and `SceneImporter.cpp` (decode).
- `ImageWriter.hpp/.cpp` — `writePNG()` wraps CoreGraphics/ImageIO's C APIs to write an RGBA8 buffer as a real PNG file. Needs the CoreGraphics/ImageIO frameworks, so unlike the rest of `Export/` it's linked only into the `RayTracerBench` app target, not the framework-free `RayTracerCore` library.

### CPU renderer (`CPU/`)

Pure C++17, no Apple frameworks (kept framework-free deliberately). Threads via plain `std::thread` row-partitioning — not GCD — with a `CPUThreading::SingleThreaded/MultiThreaded` toggle. Default is MultiThreaded (realistic CPU perf); SingleThreaded is exposed explicitly so the UI's speedup number is never ambiguous about which CPU baseline it used.

### GPU renderer (`GPU/`)

Written directly against `metal-cpp` (`MTL::Device`, `MTL::Buffer`, `MTL::ComputePipelineState`, `MTL::Texture`). Device/queue/pipeline are created once at launch and cached, so first-render shader-compile latency never pollutes timed numbers. `Shaders/Raytracer.metal` is a `kernel void renderKernel(...)` dispatched with `dispatchThreads:threadsPerThreadgroup:` (8×8 threadgroups), taking `transforms`/`shapes`/`entityCount`/`materials`/`camera`/`params` as its buffer arguments (buffers 0–5) — the entity component arrays plus materials, mirroring `CPURenderer.cpp`'s raw pointers. Buffers use `MTL::ResourceStorageModeShared` (Apple Silicon unified memory — no explicit copy-back). Two timing numbers are surfaced: wall-clock and GPU-only (`GPUStartTime()`/`GPUEndTime()`), with GPU-only as the headline metric. A throwaway warm-up dispatch always precedes a timed run.

`RasterRenderer.hpp/.cpp` + `Shaders/Raster.metal` are a third renderer for the same `SceneDescription`, using Metal's standard graphics pipeline (`MTL::RenderPipelineState` + a depth buffer) instead of a compute kernel — see "Project status" for the full design (camera-matrix derivation from `CameraGPU`'s existing fields, single-draw-call geometry batched from `Export/EntityMesh.hpp`'s `buildEntityMesh()`, the deliberately basic unlit-ish shading model, and why back-face culling is off). `Raster.metal` has no local `#include`s (unlike `Raytracer.metal`), so like `Blit.metal` it's compiled from an in-memory source string at runtime rather than needing the `xcrun`/`.metallib` build step. Same warm-up-then-timed-draw methodology and `GPUStartTime()`/`GPUEndTime()` timing as `GPURenderer`, but reports triangle count instead of rays/sec.

`CameraMath.hpp` is a small header-only, shared home for the view/perspective-projection matrix math `RasterRenderer.cpp` originally kept to itself — factored out once `PipelineStageRenderer` needed the same primitives for cameras that don't come from a `CameraGPU` (see below). `PipelineStageRenderer.hpp/.cpp` + `Shaders/Wireframe.metal` render the "Pipeline Steps" secondary window's three wireframe diagram stages (a fourth, "Viewport Matrix", is just `RasterRenderer`'s own output, reused as-is) — see "Project status" for the full design and the framing-exclusion rule that keeps the scene's radius-1000 ground sphere from dominating the diagram's auto-framed camera the way it dominated the earlier Unity-import investigation.

### UI (`App/`)

- `AppDelegate` + `main.cpp` bootstrap an `NS::ApplicationDelegate` and the main window — no Swift/Storyboard.
- Scene display uses Metal itself, not `NSImageView`: `ImageDisplayView` is a `CAMetalLayer`-backed view with a textured-quad blit shader (`Shaders/Blit.metal`), used for the CPU output (RGBA8 buffer uploaded via `replaceRegion`), the GPU (compute) output, and the Raster output (the latter two already textures) — three side-by-side instances, one per renderer.
- `ControlsPanel` exposes image-width, samples-per-pixel, max-depth, CPU-threading toggle, scene-seed field/randomize, a "Floating?" checkbox, `Render CPU` / `Render GPU` / `Render Raster` / `Compare` buttons, `Save glTF` / `Save OBJ` buttons, and a `Load Scene` button (row 1, next to Randomize — row 2 was already packed, so `Render Raster` joined `Load Scene` there for the same reason). `Compare` runs all three renderers against one freshly-built shared `SceneDescription`; the Save buttons build a scene the same way, hand it to `Export/SceneExporter.hpp`, and — on success — also render it via `renderCPU()` at the current settings and write a same-named `.png` preview via `Export/ImageWriter.hpp` into the same directory, then show the result in an `NS::Alert`. That render is why `saveScene()` runs on a background `std::thread` like the render actions above, rather than staying the fast, synchronous, main-thread-only operation it was before the preview image existed. `Load Scene` shows an `NS::OpenPanel` (see `ThirdParty/metal-cpp-extensions/AppKit/NSOpenPanel.hpp`, a project-local addition — Apple's vendored extensions have no `NSOpenPanel` wrapper either), then reconstructs the picked `.gltf`/`.obj` via `Export/SceneImporter.hpp` on a background thread; on success, `AppDelegate::sceneForRender()` makes every subsequent Render CPU/GPU/Raster/Compare (and Save) use that loaded scene's geometry/materials, with camera/params always refreshed from the current controls, until another Load Scene replaces it — see "Project status" for the full design.
- `ResultsPanel` shows CPU render time + estimated rays/sec, GPU render time (wall + GPU-only) + estimated rays/sec, Raster render time (wall + GPU-only) + triangle count, and a speedup line covering every pairwise ratio among whichever of CPU/GPU/Raster have been run.
- A `Pipeline Steps` button (row 1, the last remaining gap) opens `PipelineVisualizationWindow` — an optional secondary window with four labeled panels (its own four `ImageDisplayView`s) showing the Projective/Camera/Orthographic/Viewport pipeline stages for a scene built at the main window's current settings; see "Project status" for the full design. Its own `Refresh` button rebuilds and re-renders all four from whatever the main window's controls currently say. `AppDelegate` creates it lazily on first click and keeps it alive for the app's lifetime (`ThirdParty/metal-cpp-extensions/AppKit/NSWindow.hpp`'s new `setReleasedWhenClosed(false)` keeps that pointer valid across the user closing and reopening the window).
- Renders run on background `std::thread`s; completion marshals back to the main thread via `dispatch_async(dispatch_get_main_queue(), ...)` — GCD's plain C API, so no Objective-C is needed even for this.

## Verification approach

- **Eyeball correctness**: same shared scene seed on both renderers should produce the same sphere layout/materials/colors; per-pixel noise differences are expected since CPU/GPU RNG *streams* differ even though the RNG *function* is shared.
- **Deterministic parity**: a small fixed scene with a fixed hash-seed, run on both CPU and GPU, diffed directly — this is only meaningful because `RayTraceCore.h` is shared verbatim. Will eventually be covered by `RayTracerBenchTests/DeterministicParityTests.cpp` (not created yet — verified so far with an ad hoc standalone harness, not committed). **The originally-planned ~2/255 tolerance is optimistic and was revised after actually measuring it**: at low sample counts (spp=16, 64×36 preview), per-channel mismatches over a "2" tolerance affected ~8.8% of channels with a max diff of 61/255 — but this is not a bug. Diffs are exactly zero on every pure-sky pixel (no scatter/branching at all) and appear only where rays hit geometry, and raising spp 16→200 cut both the mismatch rate (8.8%→4.9%) and max diff (61→13) — the signature of a chaotic Monte Carlo system where a sub-ULP floating-point difference (CPU vs GPU `sqrt`/`pow`) flips a scatter/reflectance branch and the two paths diverge completely from there, rather than a systematic error that a real bug would produce (which wouldn't shrink with more samples). If writing `DeterministicParityTests.cpp`, assert on **mismatch rate at high spp**, not a strict per-pixel bound at low spp.
- **Benchmark validity**: always warm up the GPU once before timing; run each config 3× and report the min alongside the raw number; expose multiple spp presets (10/50/100/500). At small workloads GPU dispatch/readback overhead can dominate and CPU may look competitive — this is an expected artifact of the benchmark, not a bug, and should be noted rather than "fixed."
